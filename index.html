Market Signals

A small team is running a research project that tests thousands of trading ideas on years of stock market data. Every test is also run on shuffled data, to measure how often a meaningless idea would pass by luck.

Test results from 10 Oct to 20 Nov 2026. Updated 24 Nov 2026.

The short version

Of 3,883 ideas, 668 have been tested so far and 2 have held up all the way. Neither is being traded yet. At the first screen, real ideas pass about as often as luck alone would let them, so a single pass means very little. That's why ideas have to clear several stages before they count.

Real, or luck?

Each stage also runs luck-only tests on fake or shuffled data, which can only pass by chance. If real ideas pass at the same rate, those passes are mostly noise.

At the first screen, real ideas pass 2.7% of the time and luck-only tests 3.2%: no difference. The battery check only sees ideas that already passed a screen, and there real ideas pass 20.8% against 1.9% for luck. That's where real signals start to stand out.

What has held up

F01A stock's recent path predicts how much it will move next week

How long it has been since a stock's 60-day high and low, how far it sits below the high, and how much of its recent move came on a handful of big days together forecast how much it will move over the next one to two weeks. Its own past volatility doesn't capture this. It predicts the size of the move, not the direction. It held on 2021 data it was never tuned on, and it isn't explained by earnings dates or by simply being down from the high. It also shows up in Europe, more weakly.

Possible use: sizing positions or pricing options. Nothing has been built to trade it yet.

F03Recent extreme days point the same way, but it's the same signal

The number and timing of a stock's recent extreme days also forecast next week's volatility, and that held on 2021 data. Once F01 is accounted for, though, it adds nothing new, so it's treated as part of F01.

There are also 7 open leads still being checked. The clearest ones:

  • F02 European and Asian stocks follow the US move by one session, mostly right at their open. Probably too small to trade after costs.
  • F08 Thinly traded stocks, like small banks and municipal-bond funds, react to the market's move a day late.
  • F17 The recent trend in US Treasuries says something about a few municipal-bond funds and small banks.

The other 4 are narrower checks of the method itself.
